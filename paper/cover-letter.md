Dear Editor,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "Task-Scoped Data Entitlements: Deriving and Database-Enforcing
Least-Privilege Data Access for LLM Agents," for publication in IEEE Access.

LLM data agents usually query databases with their user's full credential. Recent agent-security work enforces least
privilege at the level of tools, but for a data agent the relevant tool is a single query interface, so the data the
agent can reach is unchanged. The manuscript proposes task-scoped entitlements: a trusted broker infers, from the
natural-language task, the tables, columns and rows the task needs, compiles that scope into an ephemeral PostgreSQL
role and security-barrier views owned by the user's role, and provides a bounded, audited widening request when the
inferred scope is insufficient.

The contributions are (1) a formal model of task scopes with an over-exposure metric and a design guarantee stated
under explicit trust assumptions; (2) a scope-inference procedure; (3) model-independent database enforcement;
(4) a controlled widening protocol; and (5) a preregistered evaluation on public BIRD and Spider tasks with two
commercial LLMs, open-weight models and two agent frameworks, covering over-exposure, utility, overhead, indirect
prompt injection, widening abuse (including a task-grounded approval judge that closes the main residual channel), and
scripted enforcement-bypass attempts, with a comparison against a SQL-parsing gate that enforces the same scope.

We state the load-bearing assumptions (scope-inference quality and correct database enforcement) and what the approach
does not protect against in the abstract and introduction, and we report a candid limitations section and a threat
matrix. All numbers in the paper are generated by a script from released run logs; code, synthetic policies and
preregistration are available at https://github.com/sangaraju1988/task-scoped-entitlements (MIT License; archived at Zenodo, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24578031).

The manuscript is original, has not been published, and is not under consideration elsewhere. It does not overlap with
the author's prior publications; one prior IEEE Access article by the author on a complementary topic
(DOI 10.1109/ACCESS.2026.3730363) is cited in related work. There are no conflicts of interest.
